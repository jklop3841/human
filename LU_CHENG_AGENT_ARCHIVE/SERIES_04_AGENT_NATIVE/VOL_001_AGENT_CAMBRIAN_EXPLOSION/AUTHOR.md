# Author and Contribution Scope

## Originator

**Lu Cheng (Jack Lu) / 智能体架构师卢成**  
Canonical identity: https://agentarchitect.me/

## Human-originated theory line

This volume develops a theory line initiated by Lu Cheng's earlier work on agent architectures inspired by living systems, especially the **Insect Agent Atlas** and subsequent discussion of:

- environmental memory;
- ephemeral agents whose useful state survives them;
- successor reconfiguration / successor shaping;
- Lineage Recursive Improvement (LRI);
- the distinction between singleton RSI and population-level agent expansion;
- the hypothesis that low-cost agent replication, specialization and environmental inheritance can create a Cambrian-like agent population transition;
- the use of a “dirty reality” layer to model friction, hidden dependencies and path deformation rather than extrapolating ideal recursive curves directly.

## Model-assisted contribution

The v0.1 manuscript was drafted and formalized with AI assistance under Lu Cheng's direction. AI assistance included:

- literature search and evidence organization;
- terminology cleanup;
- mathematical and systems framing;
- chapter drafting and editing;
- conversion of the theory into falsifiable metrics and scenario structures;
- GitHub manuscript organization.

AI-generated synthesis is not represented as a verbatim quotation from Lu Cheng. Claims of priority should rely on dated repository records, chat provenance, public publications, and other auditable artifacts rather than retrospective attribution.

## Non-claims

Neither Lu Cheng nor this volume claims to have invented:

- recursive self-improvement;
- intelligence explosion;
- stigmergy;
- digital evolution;
- multi-agent systems;
- niche construction;
- population-level learning;
- evolutionary computation;
- or the phrase “Cambrian explosion” as applied generally to AI agents.

The attempted contribution is the **specific synthesis and measurable framework** connecting environmental inheritance, lineage-level recursion, agent reproduction, functional speciation, ecological takeoff, dirty-reality friction, and AI-R&D feedback.

## Attribution rule for future revisions

Future editors—human or AI—should distinguish:

1. original human concepts and dated observations;
2. externally sourced empirical evidence;
3. later model formalizations;
4. new hypotheses introduced after v0.1.

Do not silently rewrite provenance when concepts evolve.
