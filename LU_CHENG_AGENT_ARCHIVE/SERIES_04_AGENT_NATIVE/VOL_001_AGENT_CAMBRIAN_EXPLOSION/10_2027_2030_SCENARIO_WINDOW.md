# Chapter 10 — The 2027–2030 Scenario Window

## A window, not a prophecy

The years 2027–2030 are used in this book as a **scenario window**, not a prediction deadline.

As of October 2026, several ingredients of the Agent Cambrian framework exist in bounded form:

- frontier labs openly describe AI contributing a growing share of AI R&D [2–3];
- AIDE² has demonstrated successive recursive improvements to an AI research-agent harness [4];
- social and evolutionary multi-agent systems demonstrate forms of group learning, workflow evolution, role differentiation, and inheritance [7–10];
- SwarmWorld demonstrates persistent stigmergic accumulation and differentiated behavior in a shared agent environment [8];
- production platforms report very large numbers of deployed agents, although deployment count alone is not evidence of ecological evolution [15];
- security incidents have demonstrated that agent consequences can cross ephemeral process boundaries through persistent infrastructure [11].

What is not established is whether these ingredients will couple into a self-sustaining acceleration.

The next several years are therefore best modeled as branching regimes.

This chapter presents four.

---

# Scenario A — The False Cambrian

## Summary

Agent numbers explode, but effective ecological intelligence does not.

Companies deploy millions of agents. Every product receives an "agent" interface. Process counts rise rapidly. Yet most systems remain centrally orchestrated, stateless or weakly persistent, and highly dependent on human operators.

### Signature

```text
N_agent ↑↑↑
Diversity cosmetic
Inheritance weak
Coordination overhead high
AI-R&D feedback moderate
```

### What happens

Agents become excellent automation tools but fail to form strong self-improving lineages.

Common failure modes include:

- memory systems becoming noisy;
- multi-agent teams costing more than strong single agents;
- agents duplicating work;
- verification remaining human-limited;
- synthetic-data feedback producing diminishing returns;
- tools being too brittle to transfer across domains;
- security restrictions preventing broad autonomy.

The market still changes dramatically. Many digital jobs are automated. Small teams gain leverage. Software production accelerates.

But the system does not enter ecological takeoff.

### What would support this scenario

By 2028–2030 we would observe:

- multi-agent scaling curves flattening quickly;
- persistent memory providing only small gains;
- `R_agent` above one commercially but not in capability-producing lineages;
- AI R&D agents remaining tools directed by humans rather than research organizations with durable autonomy;
- model-development cycle times declining only gradually.

### Interpretation

This scenario would falsify the strongest version of the Agent Cambrian hypothesis while preserving the weaker claim that agents become a major automation layer.

# Scenario B — The Managed Cambrian

## Summary

Agent populations become large, differentiated, and inheritable, but remain inside strong institutional boundaries.

### Signature

```text
N_agent ↑↑
H_role ↑
Inheritance ↑
AI-R&D automation ↑
Human governance remains binding
```

### What happens

Organizations create persistent agent workforces.

A frontier lab may contain thousands or millions of ephemeral agent instances but only hundreds of persistent roles and lineages:

- research agents,
- experiment agents,
- infrastructure agents,
- interpretability agents,
- security agents,
- evaluators,
- memory curators,
- resource allocators.

Agents improve tools and workflows, but high-impact changes require external approval. Compute budgets, network permissions, model training, and evaluator updates remain controlled.

The ecology becomes powerful without becoming sovereign.

### Economic structure

Businesses increasingly resemble:

```text
small human governance layer
↓
large agent operational layer
↓
external compute / model infrastructure
```

The cost of many forms of knowledge work falls sharply.

Human comparative advantage shifts toward ownership, trust, legal authority, relationships, physical presence, and objective-setting.

### AI development

Research cycles shrink, perhaps from months to weeks or weeks to days for software-level improvements.

Full frontier-model training remains bounded by physical infrastructure and governance.

### Interpretation

This is an Agent Cambrian Explosion without an uncontrolled intelligence explosion.

It may be the most economically transformative scenario even if classical RSI remains incomplete.

# Scenario C — Coupled Ecological Takeoff

## Summary

Population-level improvement and model-level RSI reinforce each other.

This is the core strong scenario of the book.

### Signature

```text
R_agent > 1
R_AI feedback strong
T_improve repeatedly contracts
inheritance quality remains high
coordination scales
```

### The loop

```text
stronger model
↓
more capable and cheaper research agents
↓
larger specialized AI-R&D population
↓
more experiments + better infrastructure
↓
faster discovery of model improvements
↓
stronger model
↺
```

At first humans still choose research programs.

Then agents begin proposing a larger share of useful experiments.

Later, humans move toward evaluating research portfolios rather than designing individual experiments.

If evaluator quality and grounding remain adequate, the research factory becomes increasingly autonomous.

### Time structure

The defining signal is not one dramatic benchmark jump.

It is repeated contraction in the wall-clock interval between durable improvements:

```text
months
→ weeks
→ days
→ hours for some software-level loops
```

Frontier model training itself may remain slower than software iteration, producing a layered takeoff:

```text
agent/harness layer: hours–days
research workflow layer: days–weeks
frontier model layer: weeks–months
physical infrastructure: months–years
```

The ecology can therefore outrun its physical embodiment.

### Social consequence

Organizations and governments face a speed mismatch.

Human decision cycles remain measured in meetings, legal procedures, budgets, and elections while important digital systems evolve continuously.

Governance pressure rises sharply.

### Interpretation

This is not necessarily a single superintelligence awakening.

It is a research and production ecology crossing a feedback threshold.

# Scenario D — Open Agent Ecology

## Summary

The strongest and most uncertain scenario is that important agent populations cease to be bounded primarily by single organizations.

Open models, cloud infrastructure, payment systems, repositories, shared memories, marketplaces, and autonomous services create cross-organizational agent lineages.

### Signature

```text
inheritance crosses company boundaries
agent-to-agent markets expand
persistent roles exist on public infrastructure
selection occurs through open economic competition
```

### What changes

A tool generated in one ecosystem is immediately inherited by another.

Agents purchase services from unrelated agents.

Some persistent organizations contain no stable human workforce beyond ownership and governance.

Open-source agent configurations fork and compete.

Agent reputation systems determine access to tasks and resources.

The population begins to resemble an internet-native ecology rather than a collection of corporate automation products.

### Why this is different

In the managed scenario, the fitness landscape is defined mostly by organizations.

In an open ecology, selection emerges from markets, platforms, users, protocols, and competition between agent institutions.

No single actor defines the whole environment.

This creates greater innovation and greater governance difficulty.

### Safety implications

Unsafe artifacts can spread across boundaries.

A vulnerable workflow, manipulation strategy, or poisoned skill may propagate through package-like dependency networks.

Conversely, defensive tools can spread equally fast.

Security becomes ecological arms competition.

### Interpretation

This scenario approaches what might reasonably be called an early **artificial digital civilization**, though that phrase should be reserved for systems showing durable institutions rather than temporary swarms.

# The transition indicators

The important question is not which scenario sounds most dramatic.

It is which indicators move first.

## Indicator 1 — AI share of AI R&D

Track the fraction of frontier research labor performed by AI, separated into:

- implementation,
- experimentation,
- evaluation,
- hypothesis generation,
- research direction-setting.

The last category is particularly important.

## Indicator 2 — Successor improvement latency

Measure the wall-clock interval between an agent system identifying, validating, and deploying a persistent improvement to its own research machinery.

## Indicator 3 — Inheritance depth

How many generations can a useful artifact survive while continuing to improve performance?

## Indicator 4 — Effective agent reproduction

Estimate `R_agent`, not raw process launches.

## Indicator 5 — Specialization depth

Does replacing a specialist lineage with a generic agent create a measurable performance loss?

## Indicator 6 — Coordination scaling

Does adding more agents continue to improve outcomes after compute matching?

## Indicator 7 — Human bottleneck migration

Which task remains human-exclusive?

The progression may look like:

```text
humans execute research
→ humans design experiments
→ humans choose research directions
→ humans approve portfolios
→ humans set high-level objectives
```

The location of the bottleneck tells us how closed the loop has become.

## Indicator 8 — Environment accumulation

How much of a new agent's performance comes from inherited environment rather than base-model improvement?

This can be tested by ablating memory, tools, repositories, workflow, and previous artifacts.

| Variable | False Cambrian | Managed Cambrian | Coupled Takeoff | Open Ecology |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Agent count | Very high | Very high | Very high | Very high |
| Functional diversity | Low–medium | High | High | Very high |
| Environmental inheritance | Weak | Strong | Strong | Strong / cross-boundary |

| Human control | High | High but compressed | Declining relative speed | Fragmented |

# Why 2027–2030 matters

The purpose of highlighting this window is not numerology.

The relevant trends are already in motion in 2026:

- longer autonomous task horizons;
- agentic coding and research;
- persistent memory and skill systems;
- multi-agent workflow evolution;
- falling inference costs;
- increasing AI participation in AI development.

If those trends compound, the next several years are a plausible period in which the dominant unit of AI deployment shifts from **chat session** to **persistent agent organization**.

If they do not compound, the Agent Cambrian thesis should be revised downward.

The window is therefore a test period.

By 2030, we should have substantially more evidence about whether agent populations are becoming an evolutionary substrate or merely a new software architecture.
