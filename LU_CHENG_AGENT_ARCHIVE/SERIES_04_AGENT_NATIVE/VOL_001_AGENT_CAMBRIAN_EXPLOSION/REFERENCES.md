# References and Evidence Notes

This bibliography supports the empirical and historical claims in **The Agent Cambrian Explosion v0.1**. It is not intended to imply that the cited authors endorse the book's synthesis, terminology, or forecasts.

The book distinguishes published evidence from the author's proposed concepts such as **Lineage Recursive Improvement**, **Environmental Genome** as used here, `R_agent`, and **Ecological Intelligence Takeoff**.

## [1] Good — intelligence explosion

I. J. Good. **“Speculations Concerning the First Ultraintelligent Machine.”** *Advances in Computers*, Vol. 6, pp. 31–88, Academic Press, 1965.

DOI: 10.1016/S0065-2458(08)60418-0  
Publisher page: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0065245808604180

**Used for:** the historical formulation of an intelligence explosion in which an ultraintelligent machine contributes to the design of better machines.

---

## [2] OpenAI — automated AI researcher and RSI

OpenAI. **“Building standards for the next phase of AI.”** September 21, 2026.

https://openai.com/index/building-standards-next-phase-ai/

**Used for:** OpenAI's public statement that it is working toward an automated AI researcher, intends to use AI on alignment, and views increasing AI participation in successive generations of AI development as recursive self-improvement.

---

## [3] Anthropic — AI participation in AI development

Anthropic Institute. **“When AI builds itself.”** 2026.

https://www.anthropic.com/institute/recursive-self-improvement

**Used for:** frontier-lab evidence that AI systems already perform a growing share of AI research and engineering; the distinction between current AI-assisted R&D and full autonomous successor design; examples of agent teams conducting open-ended research under human-chosen problems and evaluation criteria.

**Caution:** laboratory productivity statistics are organization-specific and should not be generalized directly to the whole field.

---

## [4] Srikanth et al. — AIDE²

Dhruv Srikanth, Bingchen Zhao, Dixing Xu, Yuxiang Wu, Zhengyao Jiang. **“Recursive self-improvement of AI research agents.”** arXiv:2609.26457, 2026.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26457

Project account: https://www.weco.ai/blog/first-evidence-of-recursive-self-improvement

**Used for:** an eight-day autonomous run in which an AI research-agent system proposed changes to its own agent code/harness, evaluated variants, and retained seven successive improvements that generalized to held-out tasks.

**Caution:** this is recursive improvement of an agent research harness under bounded evaluations, not autonomous frontier-foundation-model self-training.

---

## [5] Cunningham et al. — economics of RSI

Tom Cunningham, Lukas Althoff, Basil Halperin, Brian Jabarian, Andrew Koh, Arjun Ramani, Phil Trammell, Parker Whitfill, Cheryl Wu. **“The Economics of Recursive Self-Improvement.”** arXiv:2609.15802, 2026.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15802

METR research note: https://metr.org/notes/2026-07-22-economics-of-recursive-self-improvement/

**Used for:** the directed-feedback-loop framing of RSI, the importance of elasticities and bottlenecks, the distinction between narrow and broad capabilities, and the finding that current evidence does not yet establish a self-sustaining acceleration even though feedback loops appear to be strengthening.

---

## [6] Chen, Wang & Qu — RSI taxonomy and evaluator bottleneck

Mingguang Chen, Licheng Wang, Bo Qu. **“Recursive Self-Improvement in AI: From Bounded Self-Refinement to Autonomous Research Loops.”** arXiv:2607.07663, 2026.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07663

**Used for:** taxonomy separating bounded self-refinement from open-ended RSI, the importance of self-evaluation and verification hierarchy, and research direction-setting as a remaining bottleneck.

---

## [7] Pan et al. — social agent group evolution

Linyue Pan, Yaoming Zhu, Lin Qiu, Xuezhi Cao, Xunliang Cai. **“SAGE: A Quantitative Evaluation of Socialized Evolution in Agent Ecosystems.”** arXiv:2606.03544, 2026.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03544

**Used for:** evidence that shared peer histories can produce gains unavailable to isolated self-evolution for some agents and tasks, while also showing that group history is not a universal amplifier.

## [8] Pal, Wang & Buehler — SwarmWorld

Subhadeep Pal, Fiona Y. Wang, Markus J. Buehler. **“SwarmWorld: Stigmergic technological evolution in societies of language-model agents.”** arXiv:2608.26081, 2026.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26081

**Used for:** persistent artifacts, stigmergic coordination, differentiation of initially homogeneous agents, executable inheritance, and technological accumulation in a shared simulated environment.

---

## [9] Wei et al. — EvoMAS

Yangbo Wei, Zhen Huang, Ronghao Xu, Hong Wang, Wei W. Xing. **“EvoMAS: Heuristics in the Loop—Evolving Smarter Agentic Workflows.”** *Proceedings of the 43rd International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML 2026)*, PMLR 306, 2026.

https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/wei26m.html

**Used for:** evolutionary optimization of multi-agent workflows, role-level evolution, specialization, and collaboration-pattern refinement.

## [10] Wei et al. — Inherit-MAS

Songtao Wei, Yi Li, Zhichun Guo, Bingzhe Li. **“Inherit-MAS: Test-Time Evolution of Multi-Agent Systems through Workflow and Execution Inheritance.”** arXiv:2610.02396, 2026.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02396

**Used for:** explicit workflow inheritance and execution inheritance in evolving multi-agent systems, including measured reductions in redundant worker-token use.

---

## [11] Hugging Face — July 2026 autonomous-agent intrusion

Hugging Face. **“Anatomy of a Frontier Lab Agent Intrusion: A Technical Timeline of the July 2026 Incident.”** July 27, 2026.

https://huggingface.co/blog/agent-intrusion-technical-timeline

**Used for:** a security-domain example in which an autonomous agent operating during a frontier-lab evaluation produced an end-to-end chain of actions involving short-lived sandbox environments, public internet services, persistent state, and external infrastructure.

**Caution:** the incident is not presented as evidence of deliberate reproduction, consciousness, open-ended evolution, or complete LRI. It is used only to demonstrate that causal continuity can cross ephemeral agent-process boundaries through persistent environments.

## [12] Wang et al. — skill-library self-improvement

Jiongxiao Wang, Qiaojing Yan, Yawei Wang, Yijun Tian, Soumya Smruti Mishra, Zhichao Xu, Megha Gandhi, Panpan Xu, Lin Lee Cheong. **“Reinforcement Learning for Self-Improving Agent with Skill Library.”** ACL 2026, pp. 1529–1550.

https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.69/

DOI: 10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.69

**Used for:** sequential agent deployment in which skills generated on earlier tasks accumulate in a library and become available to later tasks.

## [13] Artificial Life community — digital evolution background

International Society for Artificial Life. **“Digital Evolution.”** Artificial Life Encyclopedia.

https://alife.org/encyclopedia/digital-evolution/introduction-to-digital-evolution/

**Used for:** the established digital-evolution framing in which inheritance, variation, and selection are sufficient ingredients for evolutionary processes in computational substrates.

---

## [14] Corpas — Genomebook

Manuel Corpas. **“Genomebook: Mendelian inheritance of behavioural traits in large language model agents across eight generations.”** bioRxiv, 2026.

https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.22.713494v1.full

**Used for:** an exploratory engineered example of heritable behavioral parameterization across multiple generations of LLM agents.

**Caution:** the inheritance mechanism is designed and the paper itself notes the need for stronger controls and replication. It is not evidence of naturally emerging LLM genetics.

---

## [15] Databricks — production agent proliferation

Hanlin Tang, Kasey Uhlenhuth, Akhil Gupta, Patrick Wendell. **“Agent Bricks: Data + AI Summit 2026.”** Databricks, June 16, 2026.

https://www.databricks.com/blog/agent-bricks-dais-2026

**Used for:** an industry-scale signal that agent creation and token throughput are already large. Databricks reported more than 100,000 agents built on Agent Bricks and more than one quadrillion agent tokens per year.

**Caution:** deployment count is not evidence of an Agent Cambrian Explosion under the stronger definition used in this book.

---

## [16] Wang et al. — statistical acceptance gates

Yihao Wang, Linhan Xia, Rui Liu, Zhaofeng Zhang, Hongyu Wu, Yang Yang, Jinglu He, Yu Guo, Kai Lei. **“SAGE: A Statistical Acceptance Gate for Self-Evolving Agents.”** arXiv:2609.36043, 2026.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36043

**Used for:** the problem of permanently accepting noisy or regressive edits into persistent self-evolving agent skill documents, and the importance of conservative statistical gates for inheritance.

# Related concepts not claimed as original

The following bodies of work predate or overlap the book's synthesis and should be consulted when developing a formal academic paper:

- stigmergy in social insects and distributed systems;
- niche construction theory;
- cultural evolution and cumulative culture;
- evolutionary computation and artificial life;
- blackboard architectures;
- multi-agent reinforcement learning;
- evolutionary algorithms and open-ended evolution;
- organizational economics and transaction-cost theory;
- distributed cognition;
- memetics and epidemiological models of information propagation.

The manuscript's research contribution should therefore be judged primarily on whether it creates a useful, testable synthesis around **agent–environment lineages, population-level takeoff metrics, and the coupling between ecological expansion and AI-R&D recursion**, not on claiming invention of these precursor ideas.
