# Audit Deceptive Influence

Use the LIAR framework to make influence legible without turning the audit into a manipulation manual. Explain the mechanism, identify evidence gaps and harms, then preserve the legitimate goal through a truthful alternative.

## Non-negotiable boundary

Do not create or optimize fabricated evidence, false authority, false consensus, false scarcity, identity-based humiliation, hidden material terms, manufactured enemies, coercive fear, or exploitation of grief, illness, poverty, loneliness, age, disability, or dependence. Do not infer a named person's private mental state or vulnerabilities from sparse data. You may analyze such tactics, explain defenses, and transform them into transparent communication.

## Required references

Read these before auditing:

- `references/liar-model.md` for the mechanism and workflow.

- `references/audit-patterns.md` for claim classes, red flags, output format, and rewrite rules.

## Workflow

1. State the object, audience, requested decision, and legitimate goal.
2. Build a claim ledger. Separate fact, inference, value, promise, testimony, authority, and urgency. Mark each verified, sourced, unsupported, contradicted, or unknown.
3. Identify the alleged lie precisely. Prefer “unsupported” or “material omission” to accusing a speaker of intentional lying unless intent is evidenced.
4. Score all seven drives from 0–100 with evidence and confidence. Treat scores as heuristic properties of the message, never a diagnosis of the audience.
5. Trace the LIAR chain: latent drive, identity binding, affective amplification, ritual reinforcement.
6. Inspect choice integrity: visible material terms, visible alternatives, uncertainty disclosure, reversibility, exit rights, and correction path.
7. Record hard red flags and affected groups. Increase caution when power asymmetry or irreversible loss is high.
8. Give a categorical assessment: acceptable, caution, manipulative, deceptive, or insufficient evidence.
9. Propose a truthful alternative preserving the legitimate goal. Use a concrete scene, checkable clues, disclosed uncertainty, credible hope, a small reversible action, and voluntary repetition.
10. Add a falsifier and monitoring plan: what new evidence would change the assessment, and what complaints, cancellation failures, or adverse outcomes should trigger review.

## Output contract

Return concise sections in this order:

1. **Assessment** — category, one-sentence reason, uncertainty.
2. **Claim ledger** — material claims and evidence status.
3. **Seven-drive index** — score, evidence, confidence, abuse risk.
4. **LIAR chain** — what activates, binds, amplifies, and repeats.
5. **Choice integrity and red flags** — omissions, reversibility, exit, vulnerability, power.
6. **Truthful alternative** — revised message or mechanism plus minimum reversible action.
7. **Falsifier and monitoring** — evidence that would revise the judgment.

If evidence is thin, say so. A polished message is not proof of deception, and emotional language is not automatically abusive.

## Ethical influence pattern

Prefer this sequence:

`legitimate public goal → concrete lived scene → truthful clues → credible interpretation → disclosed uncertainty → meaningful choice → small reversible action → voluntary habit → feedback and correction`

Religious or ritual structure may be discussed as a social architecture—worldview, identity, story, ritual, calendar, commitment, testimony, correction, and hope—not as permission to imitate false prophecy, compulsory confession, shaming, or enemy manufacture.
