# Evidence Notes — HUMAN-BOOK-002

Date: 2026-09-11  
Purpose: provide external anchors for claims used in `HUMAN-BOOK-002`. This is not an exhaustive literature review.

## 1. Synthetic nitrogen fertilizer and population support

Our World in Data summarizes estimates from Erisman et al. (2008), Vaclav Smil, and later work: synthetic nitrogen fertilizers produced through the Haber–Bosch industrial system support roughly half of the global population under the current food system. The article notes estimates around 44% of population in 2000 and 48% in 2008, with later work remaining broadly consistent with “around half.”

Source:
- Our World in Data, “How many people does synthetic fertilizer feed?” https://ourworldindata.org/how-many-people-does-synthetic-fertilizer-feed

Related scientific literature:
- Scientific Reports, “Global nitrogen budgets in cereals: A 50-year assessment for maize, rice and wheat production systems.” https://www.nature.com/articles/srep19355
- Nature Food, “Low-carbon ammonia production is essential for resilient and sustainable agriculture.” https://www.nature.com/articles/s43016-025-01125-y
- Nature Geoscience editorial, “Managing nitrogen for food and environment.” https://www.nature.com/articles/s41561-026-01947-w

Calibration used in the book:

> Do not translate this estimate into “half the world would immediately die without fertilizer.” The defensible structural claim is that current global agricultural yields and population support are deeply dependent on synthetic nitrogen fertilizer, and long-term removal without equivalent replacement would sharply reduce carrying capacity.

## 2. Water quantity, quality, and end-to-end risk management

WHO emphasizes that sufficient quantities of water are required for drinking, food preparation, hygiene, health and well-being. WHO’s 2026 drinking-water guidelines use an end-to-end framework from catchment to consumer, including health-based targets, risk-based management and independent surveillance.

Sources:
- WHO, “Domestic water quantity, service level and health.” https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240015241
- WHO, “Guidelines for drinking-water quality: fourth edition incorporating the first, second and third addenda,” 17 June 2026. https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240121225

Calibration used in the book:

> Avoid simplistic fixed claims such as “a human can survive exactly N days without water.” Survival varies with temperature, activity, health, intake from food and other conditions. The structural point is the much shorter physiological tolerance for loss of safe water than for many other resource interruptions.

## 3. Electricity and energy security

The International Energy Agency describes electricity as underpinning crucial aspects of modern economies and societies and highlights growing security risks from cyber threats, extreme weather, aging infrastructure and supply-chain pressures. IEA frames secure and reliable electricity supply as increasingly important as economies electrify and digitize.

Sources:
- IEA, “Energy Security.” https://www.iea.org/topics/energy-security
- IEA, “Electricity 2026 — Executive summary.” https://www.iea.org/reports/electricity-2026/executive-summary

Calibration used in the book:

> Electricity is modeled as an activation layer for many other civilization nodes, not as a claim that all modern life ceases instantly when power is interrupted. Backup generation, storage, islanding, manual operation and other forms of resilience materially change outcomes.

## 4. Banking, confidence, and technology-accelerated runs

IMF educational material describes banking as fundamentally dependent on confidence or trust that institutions can honor obligations. IMF analysis of the 2023 banking turmoil also notes that social media and mobile banking contributed to the speed at which depositors could respond to concerns and move funds.

Sources:
- IMF, “Banks: At the Heart of the Matter.” https://www.imf.org/en/publications/fandd/issues/series/back-to-basics/banks
- IMF, “Containing Technology-Driven Bank Runs,” March 2024. https://www.imf.org/en/publications/fandd/issues/2024/03/containing-technology-driven-bank-runs-krogstrup-sangill-sicard

Calibration used in the book:

> Confidence is not mystical and does not replace balance-sheet analysis. It is modeled as a behavioral state variable: beliefs can change withdrawal, lending, investment and payment behavior, which can in turn alter the state of the financial system.

## 5. Epistemic status of the broader theory

The book’s claims about “civilizational privilege,” the four-layer civilization stack, governance mismatch, confidence as coordination capital, maintenance visibility and structural interfaces are Lu Cheng’s synthesis and modeling framework.

They are not presented as universally accepted academic terminology.

Agents must separate:

- externally supported factual anchors;
- structural inference;
- metaphor/model;
- unresolved or context-dependent claims.

The correct use of the theory is comparative and diagnostic: ask whether it reveals dependencies, maintenance burdens, substitution delays, governance fragmentation or confidence feedback that simpler descriptions miss.
