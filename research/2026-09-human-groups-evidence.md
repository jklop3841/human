# Evidence Calibration — Human Groups and High-Permission Institutions

Date: 2026-09-11  
Related: `HUMAN-SOCIETY-002`, `HUMAN-BOOK-003`

This file separates external research from Lu Cheng's structural interpretation. The book's terms such as "high-permission institution" and "institutional amplification" are analytic language used in the Human repository, not claims that these are standard names in social psychology or sociology.

## 1. Modern crowd psychology rejects the simple "mindless crowd" model

### Source
John Drury, "The Psychology of Crowd Behavior," Annual Review of Psychology 77 (2026), 339–364. DOI: 10.1146/annurev-psych-012125-121447. PubMed PMID 40763250.

### Calibration
Modern crowd research emphasizes social identity and group norms. Crowd behavior cannot be reduced to individuals losing rationality merely because they are in a crowd.

### Repository inference
Use:

`group immersion → identity/norm structure changes behavior`

Do not use:

`crowd size → mindlessness`

## 2. Social learning can produce both information aggregation and information cascades

### Source
Sushil Bikhchandani, David Hirshleifer, Omer Tamuz, Ivo Welch, "Information Cascades and Social Learning," Journal of Economic Literature 62(3), 2024, 1040–1093. DOI: 10.1257/jel.20241472.

### Calibration
Observing others can improve learning, but can also create inaccurate, fragile mass behavior and block later learning under information cascades.

### Repository inference
A large behavioral majority must not be counted as an equal number of independent observations.

## 3. Hidden-profile research: groups often fail to pool unique information

### Source
Li Lu, Y. Connie Yuan, Poppy Lauretta McLeod, "Twenty-five years of hidden profiles in group decision making: a meta-analysis," Personality and Social Psychology Review 16(1), 2012, 54–75. DOI: 10.1177/1088868311417243. PMID 21896790.

### Findings useful for this book
The meta-analysis covered 65 studies / 3,189 groups. Groups discussed substantially more common information than unique information; hidden-profile groups were much less likely to reach the optimal solution than full-information groups.

### Repository inference
`collective knowledge != discussed knowledge`

## 4. Dissent can improve information processing

### Source
Stefan Schulz-Hardt et al., "Group decision making in hidden profile situations: dissent as a facilitator for decision quality," Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 91(6), 2006, 1080–1093. DOI: 10.1037/0022-3514.91.6.1080. PMID 17144766.

### Calibration
Prediscussion dissent improved hidden-profile solution rates by increasing discussion intensity and reducing discussion bias. Dissent could be useful even when the dissenting initial preference was not itself correct.

### Repository inference
Dissent is not only a competing answer; it can function as an information-search mechanism.

## 5. Pluralistic ignorance can stabilize suboptimal norms

### Source
Sergey Gavrilets, Johannes Karl, Michele Gelfand et al., "The cultural evolution of pluralistic ignorance," Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 2026. DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2522998123. PMID 41686449.

### Calibration
Pluralistic ignorance is systematic misperception of others' attitudes and can entrench suboptimal norms.

### Repository inference
A public norm may persist even when private support is much lower than members believe.

## 6. Bystander effect / diffusion of responsibility

### Source
Peter Fischer et al., "The bystander-effect: a meta-analytic review on bystander intervention in dangerous and non-dangerous emergencies," Psychological Bulletin 137(4), 2011, 517–537. DOI: 10.1037/a0023304. PMID 21534650.

### Calibration
Across a large literature, the presence of other people can reduce individual helping in some critical situations. The effect is moderated by context and should not be treated as universal.

### Repository inference
Group size can change perceived responsibility ownership.

## 7. Authority, conformity and obedience are more complex than direct commands

### Sources
Robert Spillane, Jean-Etienne Joullié, "Authority, conformity and obedience: Applying Friedrich's theory of authority to the classics," British Journal of Social Psychology 61(4), 2022, 1086–1100. DOI: 10.1111/bjso.12527. PMID 35178725.

Stephen Gibson, "Obedience without orders: Expanding social psychology's conception of 'obedience'," British Journal of Social Psychology, 2019. PMID 30156301.

### Calibration
Authority, legitimacy, power and obedience should not be collapsed into one variable. Compliance can occur through identification with roles, institutional aims and legitimate authority, not only direct commands.

### Repository inference
Analyze the authority structure, not just explicit orders.

## 8. Organizational voice and silence

### Sources
Elizabeth W. Morrison, "Employee Voice and Silence," Annual Review of Organizational Psychology and Organizational Behavior 1, 2014, 173–197. DOI: 10.1146/annurev-orgpsych-031413-091328.

Elizabeth W. Morrison, "Employee Voice and Silence: Taking Stock a Decade Later," Annual Review of Organizational Psychology and Organizational Behavior, 2023.

### Calibration
Members can possess useful concerns or information yet withhold it because of motivational, relational or organizational conditions. Silence can deprive organizations of useful information.

### Repository inference
`local knowledge exists` does not imply `central authority receives it`.

## 9. Group performance can show both gains and losses

### Sources
Garold Stasser, Susanne Abele, "Collective Choice, Collaboration, and Communication," Annual Review of Psychology 71, 2020, 589–612. DOI: 10.1146/annurev-psych-010418-103211. PMID 31518524.

Norbert L. Kerr, R. Scott Tindale, "Group performance and decision making," Annual Review of Psychology 55, 2004, 623–655. DOI: 10.1146/annurev.psych.55.090902.142009. PMID 14744229.

### Calibration
Groups are capable of process gains and losses. Collaboration can create synergy, but information and motivation structures strongly affect performance.

### Repository inference
Never use a single rule such as `group > individual` or `individual > group`.

## 10. Collective intelligence depends on information aggregation structure

### Source
Tatsuya Kameda, Wataru Toyokawa, R. Scott Tindale, "Information aggregation and collective intelligence beyond the wisdom of crowds," Nature Reviews Psychology 1, 2022, 345–357.

### Calibration
Collective decision-making can outperform individuals under certain cognitive, informational and behavioral mechanisms.

### Repository inference
The useful variable is not number of participants alone but diversity, partial independence, aggregation and communication structure.

## 11. Network structure matters

### Source
Matthew O. Jackson, Brian W. Rogers, Yves Zenou, "The Economic Consequences of Social-Network Structure," Journal of Economic Literature 55(1), 2017, 49–95. DOI: 10.1257/jel.20150694.

### Calibration
Network macro- and micro-structure can affect learning, diffusion, decisions and behavior.

### Repository inference
Content alone is insufficient to explain collective spread; network topology must be modeled.

## 12. Intergroup threat is associated with negative outgroup attitudes

### Source
Blake M. Riek, Eric W. Mania, Samuel L. Gaertner, "Intergroup threat and outgroup attitudes: a meta-analytic review," Personality and Social Psychology Review 10(4), 2006, 336–353. DOI: 10.1207/s15327957pspr1004_4. PMID 17201592.

### Calibration
Multiple forms of perceived intergroup threat are positively related to negative outgroup attitudes.

### Repository inference
Threat perception can change the group's attitude function; it should not be treated as neutral information processing.

## 13. Groupthink requires caution

### Sources
J. K. Esser, "Alive and Well after 25 Years: A Review of Groupthink Research," Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes 73(2–3), 1998, 116–141. DOI: 10.1006/obhd.1998.2758. PMID 9705799.

S. R. Fuller, R. J. Aldag, "Organizational Tonypandy: Lessons from a Quarter Century of the Groupthink Phenomenon," Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes 73(2–3), 1998, 163–184. DOI: 10.1006/obhd.1998.2760. PMID 9705801.

### Calibration
Groupthink has been influential and heuristically valuable, but the classic model has also received substantial empirical and conceptual criticism.

### Repository inference
Use specific measurable mechanisms—dissent cost, leader anchoring, information search, premature consensus—rather than diagnosing every institutional failure as groupthink.

## 14. Bureaucracy, delegation and information asymmetry

### Sources
Erin Metz McDonnell, "Bureaucracy in Action: The Sociology of Public Administration," Annual Review of Sociology 51, 2025, 191–211. DOI: 10.1146/annurev-soc-091324-053657.

Sean Gailmard, John W. Patty, "Formal Models of Bureaucracy," Annual Review of Political Science 15, 2012, 353–377. DOI: 10.1146/annurev-polisci-031710-103314.

### Calibration
Bureaucracies combine expertise, hierarchy, delegation, information asymmetry and principal-agent problems. They can create durable capacity but also distinctive information and accountability problems.

### Repository inference
High-permission institutions should be analyzed as internally heterogeneous organizations, not personified as a single mind.

## External-fact boundary

The following are Lu Cheng / Human repository structural syntheses rather than standardized academic constructs:

- `high-permission institution`;
- `institutional amplification` as used in this book;
- the three-book layering of individual → civilization → group/institution;
- the eight-dimensional collective analysis object;
- the claim that institutions are "time extenders" and "permission amplifiers" of groups.

These are interpretive models built on, but not identical to, the external literatures above.
